In the world of AI-driven healthcare, one size does not fit all. A recent case study involving ChatGPT-4, a large language model, revealed startling disparities in medical recommendations. The study, conducted in 2025, showed that the AI's treatment suggestions varied significantly based on patients' age, gender, and race, particularly in the field of nephrology. 

Sadly, this kind of bias isn't just a theoretical problem. It has real-world impact, potentially affecting the quality of care received by individuals from diverse demographic profiles. With the increasing reliance on AI tools in healthcare, these biases could perpetuate and amplify existing disparities, leading to poorer health outcomes for marginalized groups.

We, as ethical AI practitioners and tech leaders, need to address this issue head-on. It’s time we transitioned from merely acknowledging these biases to actively working on mitigating them.
